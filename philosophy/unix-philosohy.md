# Unix Philosophy

The following 17 rules are Eric S. Raymond's summary in [The Art of Unix Programming](https://web.archive.org/web/20050317014053/http://www.faqs.org/docs/artu/), specifically its chapter on the [Basics of the Unix Philosophy](https://web.archive.org/web/20050317014053/http://www.faqs.org/docs/artu/ch01s06.html):

1. **Modularity:** Write simple parts connected by clean interfaces.
2. **Clarity:** Prefer clarity to cleverness.
3. **Composition:** Design programs to connect with other programs.
4. **Separation:** Separate policy from mechanism; interfaces from engines.
5. **Simplicity:** Add complexity only where you must.
6. **Parsimony:** Write a big program only when nothing smaller will do.
7. **Transparency:** Make inspection and debugging easy.
8. **Robustness:** Build robustness from transparency and simplicity.
9. **Representation:** Put knowledge into data so logic can stay simple.
10. **Least surprise:** Make interfaces behave as users expect.
11. **Silence:** Say nothing when there is nothing useful to say.
12. **Repair:** Repair what you can; otherwise fail noisily and quickly.
13. **Economy:** Conserve programmer time over machine time.
14. **Generation:** Automate repetitive work; have programs write programs.
15. **Optimization:** Prototype first; optimize after measuring.
16. **Diversity:** Distrust claims of one true way.
17. **Extensibility:** Design for the future without overengineering today.
